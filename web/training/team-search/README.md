# Team Rankings — technical reference (full-team neural network)

Team Rankings searches for the strongest three-Pokémon Great League teams, with their movesets, against a modelled distribution of opponent teams. It simulates complete 3-v-3 battles with PvPoke's engine under an observation-limited controller, learns a neural matchup model from those outcomes to decide which teams are worth simulating next, and then tests its finalists on opponent panels that never touched the training. The site's **PvPoke Pro › Team Rankings** tab shows the result: every evaluated team, its scores at each stage, the search's learning curves, and a deterministic replay of any saved battle.

This is the technical reference and the run guide. A short plain-language summary is in [README-plain.md](README-plain.md). Detailed measurements live beside this file: [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md) (simulator and fitting speed), [EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md](EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md) (how to tell an improvement from noise), [REFINEMENT_FINDINGS.md](REFINEMENT_FINDINGS.md) (acquisition benchmark), [PILOT_FINDINGS.md](PILOT_FINDINGS.md), [MODEL_AUDIT.md](MODEL_AUDIT.md), the controller's [information boundary](../next/OBSERVATION_CONTROLLER.md) and the [next-run audit](../next/NEXT_RUN_AUDIT.md). This revision describes the code as of 2026-10-12.

> **Status in one paragraph.** The published v1 run (October 9–10, 2026) selected Melmetal / Jumpluff / Shadow Quagsire, which scored 68.3% on 512 untouched opponent fixtures and beat the best simple baseline group by 17.6 points (paired interval 14.1–21.1). Its surrogate cut held-out team-prediction error by 77% against predicting the mean. Since then the controller was changed to see only what a player sees (v4), the search acquisition was improved by a measured 18.8 points of proposal quality, and the opponent meta can now be the teams observed in harvested YouTube battles. The first full v4 run against the observed meta (2026-10-12) selected Shadow Talonflame / Mandibuzz / Bastiodon at 83.5% on 512 untouched fixtures, with all seven other finalists inside the unresolved band. All scores are simulated expected scores under the modelled meta and controller, not human win rates, and scores under different metas are not comparable with each other (section 9).

## 1. Purpose, goals and design principles

### The problem

A team is three ordered Pokémon (the lead matters) from a pool of 300 eligible entries, each with up to five movesets: about 1.65 billion legal lead-plus-bench combinations. A single full 3-v-3 battle costs tens of milliseconds of branching JavaScript, and one battle is one noisy draw (win, draw or loss) against one opponent team, so a team's expected score needs dozens of battles before it means anything. Exhaustive search is impossible; so is evaluating more than a few thousand teams properly in a night. The system therefore needs a cheap model that predicts which teams are worth the expensive simulation, a search that uses it without being fooled by it, and an evaluation that cannot be contaminated by the search.

### Two goals, measured separately

| Goal | What is measured | How | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Accuracy** | (a) the surrogate's prediction error on team identities it never fitted; (b) the selected team's score on opponent fixtures never used in training, against fixed baselines with paired intervals; (c) the realism of the target: opponent distribution and controller | held-out diagnostics every round; sealed screening and final panels with fresh seeds; counterfactual tests on the controller; stress tests across metas | sections 6, 8, 9 |
| **Speed** | battles per second across workers, seconds per neural fit and per 12,000-candidate inference, battles spent per training label, wall time to a trustworthy result within the budget | `benchmark-simulator-speed.cjs`, `benchmark-neural-speed.cjs`, run telemetry in `checkpoint.json` | section 10 |

### Design principles, and why

1. **Labels come only from simulation, and say so.** Every score is a seeded battle outcome under a pinned engine, data snapshot and controller. The report never presents them as player win rates. Changing the controller changes the labels, so old labels are never reused across controller versions (fingerprints enforce this).
2. **The surrogate guides, the simulator decides.** The neural model only chooses what to simulate next. Every number shown about a team is a simulated score; the model's forecast is shown separately as a forecast.
3. **Sealed evaluation.** Screening and final opponent panels use seeds generated by the launcher and reserved before the search starts; they never enter fitting or proposal. The primary team is chosen by screening *before* the final test, so the headline number is not the best of several looks.
4. **Paired comparisons, not raw averages.** Teams compared on the same opponent fixtures and seeds; intervals come from resampling whole fixtures, which keeps the correlation between a team's two orientations of one opponent.
5. **Determinism and replay.** Seeds, mechanics hash, data hash and algorithm hash are recorded; the browser reruns any saved battle with the same engine and verifies the saved score.
6. **CPU, not GPU.** The expensive work is a branching battle simulator, not a tensor workload; the small network trains in milliseconds. Twelve isolated worker processes with bounded heaps and regular recycling are the right tool.

### Running a search

From the `web/` folder, with Node 20 or later and no npm packages:

```
node --test training/*.test.cjs training/next/*.test.cjs training/team-search/*.test.cjs
node training/team-search/prepare.cjs --shortlist 10000 --scout-opponents 300 --workers 12   # once; or copy an existing pool.json
node training/team-search/launch-next.cjs --hours 8                                           # detached, caffeinated, all cores
node training/team-search/status.cjs training/runs/team-search-v4
node training/team-search/stop.cjs training/runs/team-search-v4                               # drains active battles, then saves
```

With the observed meta (the exporter prints the as-of date and the prior strength that make observed teams 75% of the opponents):

```
python3 -I scripts/yt-harvest/usage_export.py
node training/team-search/launch-next.cjs --hours 8 --out training/runs/team-search-v4-observed-YYYY-MM-DD \
  --usage-file "$HOME/Desktop/Projects/PoGo Recorder/src/yt-harvest/usage/observed-gl-opponents.json" \
  --usage-half-life-days 30 --usage-as-of YYYY-MM-DD --usage-prior-strength N
```

The export also records the moves the harvest saw. Add `--observed-moves` to let the observed opponents play them, and `--alternate-panel 64` to test the finalists against the pool's alternate movesets afterwards (sections 4 and 8); both are off by default.

When the run finishes, import `training/runs/<run>/report.pvpteams.json.gz` in PvPoke Pro › Team Rankings. Runs stay local in `training/runs/`, which git ignores.

`launch-next.cjs` also tests the earlier winners: the published v1 and v3 primaries and the primary of every completed run with the same pool hash (final panel of at least 256 fixtures) join the final panel as the fixed `previous-champions` baseline group. `--champions FILE[,FILE…]` replaces that list, `--champions none` disables it, and `--champions-scope finalists` takes every final-tested team of each source (section 8, [EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md](EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md)).

## 3. The search space: eligible Pokémon and movesets

1. **Population.** The published Great League rankings with PvPoke's default matchup weights; entries sorted by weight (ties by rank), the top 300 with positive weight kept. Shadow and regional forms are separate entries; a team cannot contain two entries with the same Pokédex number (Species Clause). The current pool has 215 distinct Pokédex numbers, no unreleased or Mega entries, and total weight 964. *Why weight rather than rank:* weight is PvPoke's estimate of how often an entry is met, so the pool covers what teams must beat as well as what might be strong.
2. **Movesets.** Every legal fast/charged combination from the engine's own pools, including allowed special moves: 12,335 combinations. Each is scouted 1-v-1 against the 300 recommended-moveset opponents (excluding its own species) across the five published shield/energy scenarios: 18,440,825 single-Pokémon simulations, run in chunks of 16 movesets with a 120-second watchdog and atomic cache files, so preparation restarts from completed chunks.
3. **Retention.** Combinations are ranked by weighted mean raw battle rating and the top five kept: 1,498 variants (299 species have five, one has three). Recommended movesets land in the top five for 290 species but are not forced in. *Why five:* the candidate space is already 1.65 billion teams with five variants; more variants multiply it without changing the fact that only a few thousand teams can be simulated. *The cost:* retention is by 1-v-1 performance, so a moveset that is only good in a particular team context can be excluded (section 12).
4. **Features for the model** are computed once per variant at this stage: the scout profile against the first 24 scouted opponents and the best/worst coverage per type.

A cheaper approximate mode (`--shortlist 64 --scout-opponents 24`) projects type-aware cycle damage first and simulates only the shortlist; the overnight runs use the exhaustive pool.

## 4. The opponent meta

The search finds a best response to whatever opponents it is given, so the opponent distribution is the largest single assumption in the system. `meta.cjs` builds it from up to three components (`--meta hybrid`, the default):

- **Independent draws** from PvPoke weights raised to the power 1.25. The weight-1 floor gave 220 fringe entries 22.8% of all first-slot draws; the exponent lowers that to 13.1%. This is a popularity prior, not usage.
- **Coherent draws**, half of the synthetic share: after a weighted lead, each next member is drawn with probability proportional to weight × exp(3 × coverage improvement over the team so far), and in 40% of these teams the backline prefers a shared type that differs from the lead's (an ABB shape, multipliers 2.5 and 0.6). These are plausible patterns, not measured cores.
- **Observed teams** from the YouTube harvest (`--usage-file`). `usage_export.py` writes the opponents' complete Great League teams: open Great League and Mega Edition only; no Team Rocket, championship, no-footage or obscured videos; complete teams only, every member mapped into the pool; Shadow only when a reviewer or the Shadow classifier's held-out gate confirmed it; dated by recording date, else upload date; split and re-uploaded battles removed; each channel's rows weighted min(1, K/n) so no channel holds more than 10% of the weight. Observations decay with `--usage-half-life-days` from `--usage-as-of`, and the effective sample size is the Kish estimate (sum w)² / sum w². The observed share is `min(0.8, W / (W + prior strength))`, where W is the decayed, weighted total: a large stale archive must not dominate current play, and sparse observations are shrunk toward the prior. By default opponents use the published recommended movesets. With `--observed-moves` the observed component plays the movesets the harvest recorded (below); nothing is inferred beyond what the footage showed.

The first export (2026-10-11) gave 190 opponent teams (181 distinct) from 46 videos by 18 creators, dated 2026-09-20 to 2026-10-11; of 965 battles at 1,500 CP, 20% were eligible (52% were restricted cups, 17% partial teams, 6% unmapped species, mostly Megas). Known biases: these are top-tier players' opponents; 43% of members had an unknown Shadow status and count as the base form; most teams were seen once. The run's `meta.description` records source, hash, share, distinct teams, creators and date range, and the report shows them.

*Stratifying the observed data.* `usage_export.py --since YYYY-MM-DD --until YYYY-MM-DD` (inclusive) keeps only teams dated inside a window, by the same date rule as above; it runs after the repeated-battle rule (so which copy of a duplicated battle is kept does not depend on the window) and before the creator cap, and the quality report counts the dropped teams as `date-range`. There is **no rating filter**: no battle or video record in the catalog carries the opponent's rating or a rank bracket (the result screen is not read for it); the only rating information is free text in some video titles ("2864 ELO"), which is the creator's own rating at that moment and not the opponent's, so rating strata cannot be built from this data. The file's `rank` text says that rating is not measured.

*Observed movesets (`--observed-moves`; default off, then the file's `movesets` are ignored and everything is as before).* **Export.** `usage_export.py` adds `movesets` to a row: per member, lead first, `{fast, charged}` or null. The fast move is a human answer, else a model-resolved answer (`observed.model_answers`, which passed the held-out gate), else the extractor's top inference when its probability is at least .90, else null. The charged moves are those the battle's `move` events credit to that Pokémon, in order of first use, at most two (three distinct moves for one member is a detection error and it keeps none). Every move must be a Game Master move of the right kind and legal for the exported species (the vendor's pool rules, Return and Frustration included); others are dropped and counted. Rows that differ only in their movesets are separate rows, and a row with no usable move carries no field. Coverage of the 2026-10-13 export (783 eligible opponent teams, 2,349 members, 782 rows; the same teams, dates, creators and counts as without movesets): an observed fast move for 1,774 members (75.5%: 1,650 extractor inferences, 99 model-resolved, 25 reviewer answers), at least one charged move for 1,069 (45.5%), both for 850 (36.2%). The observed fast move differs from the recommended one for 53 of the 1,774 (3.0%), a charged move outside the recommended pair appears for 111 of the 1,069 (10.4%), and 163 members (6.9%) differ in some way, in 150 of the 783 teams (19.2%). Dropped: 575 fast-move inferences under .90 (24% of those offered), one illegal and one contradictory charged move. The 3.0% is the extractor's output, not a measured truth: its fast-move inference is a likelihood over the species' legal moves and how far it leans on the recommendation has not been measured. **Trainer.** `loadUsage` validates each move against the species' legal pools (a bad file stops the launch) and completes missing slots from the recommended moveset: the observed fast move or the recommended one; the observed charged moves, then the first recommended charged moves not already present. A member whose result equals the recommendation plays the plain row. Rows with the same species and different movesets are separate opponents with their own weight (counted once in `distinctTeams`, which stays per species team). Only the observed component changes: its fixture rows carry the moveset (`team[].moveset`, which `engine-v4-core.cjs` selects), flagged `observedMoveset`, with the same species, order and seeds as without the option, in training and in every sealed panel alike because they all draw from this meta. Independent and coherent draws, and the sensitivity metas (built from the usage file without the option, so they are the recommended-moveset alternative), keep recommended movesets. **Identity.** A member with observed moves is `speciesId@FAST/CHARGED/CHARGED` in `acquisition.cjs` `opponentIdentity` and in the stored observation, so fixtures with different movesets never share a key, evidence entry, overlap check or merge; fixtures without observed moves keep exactly their old identity. **Model.** The network encodes an opponent from its species row, which holds the recommended moveset's features. For an observed moveset the pool kept as a variant (37 of the 70 distinct observed movesets in this export) that variant's scouted features are used exactly. Otherwise the 48 of 97 per-member numbers that depend on the moves (fast and charged move types, damage and energy per turn, durations, costs, powers, buff chances) are recomputed from the moves, and the 24-opponent scout profile and the per-type coverage stay the recommended moveset's: an approximation, because the new moveset's matchups are not simulated. The recomputation reproduces the pool's stored features exactly for 90.5% of its 1,798 species and variant rows, up to the order of the two charged-move slots for another 8.8%, and differs for 0.7% (forms whose moves change with the form). It only steers which teams are worth simulating; it never enters a score. **Belief prior.** The controller's belief prior (`beliefRoster`, section 5) stays the recommended movesets: it stands for what a player knows before a move is used, public information, whereas the observed set of this fixture is exactly what the opponent will do; the 2,048-lineup bank comes from the same meta, so it would also contain it. As for a human, an unexpected move is a surprise until it is used. **Record.** `meta.description` says `Observed movesets where the usage file records them…` and carries `observedMoves`, weighted by the decayed appearance counts the sampler uses: fast move for 75.6% of members, a charged move for 47.0%, a differing moveset for 6.9% and in 19.3% of the observed team draws, and the exact and approximated feature counts. The portable report appends an opponent row for each observed (species, moveset), so the browser replays the moves a battle used (60 of 60 saved matches of a smoke run, 40 of them against observed-moveset opponents, re-simulated from the report alone to their stored scores). The alternate-moveset panel (section 8) tests the opposite question: how a finalist fares when the opponents carry something else.

A separate, independently generated 2,048-lineup bank from the same meta serves as the controller's belief prior over hidden backlines (section 5); it contains only public prior data, never the actual fixture.

*Meta-sensitivity panel.* The search's primary is a best response to the one meta above, so its final score is conditional on that assumption. After the final test, `--sensitivity-fixtures N` (default 128; 0 disables) re-simulates the final-tested finalists (`--sensitivity-groups all` adds the baseline and previous-champion teams) under K alternative metas, each on its own sealed panel of N fixtures shared by every team: independent draws at weight power 1.25, 1.0 and 1.5 (which isolates the popularity assumption), coherent draws only, and, when a usage file is given, observed teams only and observed teams without the largest creator (a leave-one-creator-out test; reports carry the removed creator's weight share and a short hash, never the name, which only the standalone tool prints on its console). `meta.cjs` builds them (`--meta coherent` is a new mode). The panel seeds derive from the validation seed like the screening seed, are guarded against the validation, screening, retest and stream seeds and against each other, and are checked against absorbed evaluation evidence and inherited training fixtures. The controller keeps the run's belief prior, so only the opponents change.

Per team and meta the report records the score and standard error, the rank among the listed teams, and across metas the mean, min, **spread (max − min)**, a bootstrap interval for the spread, and the spread that fixture noise alone would produce if the team scored the same everywhere (roughly 7 to 9 points at 128 fixtures per meta and 4 to 5 at 512, depending on the number of metas, so read a spread against it). It is a read-out: the primary stays the screening winner. A robust objective, for example mean minus spread (recorded per team as `meanMinusSpread`), is future work and is not used for selection. The data to evaluate it are kept: every team's per-fixture scores on every meta are in the checkpoint (`sensitivityScores`), and `sensitivity.cjs` can re-run the panel for any finished run. A fair test needs enough fixtures per meta (512, where noise alone is about 4 points) and replicated panels, because on the completed v4 run at 64 fixtures per meta the spread of all eight finalists was indistinguishable from fixture noise (mean spread 7.3 points against a noise floor of 7.4) and an ordering by mean minus spread at that size would mostly reflect noise. The budget reserves time for the panel (`budget.cjs`), so it moves the end of training earlier and never shortens screening or the final panel; a deadline during the panel keeps the same completed prefix on every meta and the report says it is partial. `sensitivity.cjs RUN_OR_REPORT` re-runs the panel for a finished run's finalists (section 13); [findings/meta-sensitivity.md](findings/meta-sensitivity.md) has the design choices, the first measurements and the full-scale commands.

## 5. The battle simulator and controller

![The observation-limited controller: what each side sees, what it must imagine, and how it decides](docs/teams-controller.svg)

### Engine

Every battle runs PvPoke's own `Battle.step` state machine (vendor files pinned at commit `f627e89`, MIT) inside a fresh VM context per encounter, with a virtual scheduler that advances timers without waiting for animations. Headless mode skips animation objects and detailed timeline events while keeping the compact charged events the switch clock needs; traced replays reconstruct the full timeline. Caches of type tables, move metadata and public stat templates are bounded or weakly referenced and never hold hidden live opponent state. The optimised path was verified against the baseline on every outcome, turn count, remaining Pokémon, charged count and planner statistic across 1,536 battles, with frozen pre-optimisation reference tests comparing public observations, every HP sample and every traced event. It is 1.33× faster (12.24 → 16.25 battles/s on 12 workers for the benchmark fixture). Forecast reuse (2026-10-13) goes further on the same optimised path:

- each duel forecast is simulated once per step and shared by the belief worlds that imagine it;
- bench and outgoing clones are built once per captured row and only read afterwards;
- guessed-opponent clones are kept across steps, with only HP, energy and cooldown copied in;
- the VM global is an ordinary one (`DONT_CONTEXTIFY`) instead of an interceptor-backed sandbox;
- the per-encounter Game Master is re-parsed instead of cloned.

These changes were verified event for event against the baseline path on 108 search-shaped fixtures and reproduce 192 stored outcomes of the 2026-10-12 run. At smoke scale they ran 1.9–2.0× the previous optimised path's battles per second (4 workers, shared machine); the 12-worker figure is pending ([findings](findings/speed-forecast.md)).

### Side and bench invariance (v2)

The lead is meaningful; the two bench members are canonicalised by identity for the simulator, deduplication and the model's features. Physical player slots are assigned from team identity and a seeded coin so that lexical identity never gets a privileged slot; equal-attack charged-move ties use a seeded identity-based break; both players have equal base priority; one canonical battle per fixture supplies one label (no redundant mirrored simulation). Identical teams on both sides are recorded as the expectation 0.5 of the seeded battle and its mirror.

### The observation-limited controller (v4)

PvPoke's Champion AI reads the opponent's real moves and energy. A team scored under full information can win on counter-switches no human could make, so a full-information ranking rewards the wrong teams. v4 draws the line at what a player sees:

- **Visible:** own roster and resources; opponent species that have appeared and their forms; the opponent HP bar rounded to 1%; public shield counts and switch clocks; moves the opponent has actually used, recorded after they resolve (a shielded charged move is revealed, but not to the shield decision it was shielded by); an energy estimate from counted fast and charged moves.
- **Withheld:** unrevealed backline Pokémon and their moves; the opponent's exact energy, IVs, stats and maximum HP; queued actions; the charged move awaiting a shield decision. Tests change hidden state and verify that decisions and observation records stay identical; poisoned hidden arguments throw on any property access.
- **Beliefs:** unknown moves start as the published recommended set and are overridden by observed moves; hidden backlines are three sampled lineups from the 2,048-team bank filtered to the revealed species (falling back to weighted marginal draws, which loses joint correlations); unknown IVs use default initialisation.
- **Decisions:** switching compares staying with each living bench member every four turns (sooner at low HP or when forced), rolling each imagined lineup 24 simulator steps forward against the opponent's legal replies, taking the worst-case reply per lineup and averaging over lineups; a voluntary switch needs an improvement of at least 0.08. The terminal value is remaining HP × (1 + 0.18 × energy/100) of living members, plus 0.32 per shield, plus 0.24 × a roster-coverage term (damage-per-energy pressure of survivors against the remaining opposing roster through type effectiveness and current stats), minus 0.12 × switch-lock cost. Attack choice prefers cheaper charged moves while the opponent has shields, avoids charged overkill when a fast move finishes, and otherwise favours energy-efficient or lethal moves against the predicted opponent; the shield decision averages the predicted damage of affordable opposing charged moves and shields when it reaches 35% of HP or is lethal.
- **Options (opt-in since 2026-10-13; defaults byte-identical):** `--reply-model weighted` replaces the worst-case reply with an exact softmax-weighted expectation over the opponent's legal replies, weighted by each reply's value for the opponent (the rollout value negated) at temperature `--reply-temperature` (default 0.25). `--rollout-continuation N` (at most 24) continues a forecast whose duel ends in a faint: each legal forced replacement plays up to N more steps, the replacing side keeps the best one for itself, and the terminal value then sees the next matchup instead of a knocked-out pair (at most 2 × 2 continuation duels per branch, counted in the planner statistics). Per side, for evaluation only, a switch margin (0 = eager, 1e9 = never switch voluntarily) and a shield policy (always or never). Rationale, limits and the information-boundary tests are in [the controller's option section](../next/OBSERVATION_CONTROLLER.md#controller-options); measurements are in [findings/controller.md](findings/controller.md).
- **Policy-diversity panel (`--policy-panel N`, or `../next/policy-panel.cjs` on any finished run):** after the final test, the finalists replay N common fixtures once per opponent style (default, weighted replies, no voluntary switches, eager switches, always shield, never shield) while keeping the run's controller. Each team gets a score per style, the mean, the lowest and the spread with a simultaneous paired band; the report field is `policyPanel`. It is a realism check and never affects selection.

*What this does and does not establish.* v4 is a realism correction, not a proven strength increase: a 128-fixture parity check between the lookahead and the original controller gave 50.8% (interval 45.2–56.3%). Both teams in every battle use the same controller, so neither side has an information advantage. Labels from v1–v3 cannot warm-start v4. The two options are exploratory: neither is shown to be stronger or more human-like (findings/controller.md). Still open: delayed catches, mixed strategies, continuation beyond one replacement, a learned terminal value, a reply temperature fitted to human choices, realistic counting errors and lag, and stronger or human-derived policies for the panel.

### Fingerprints

`mechanicsHash` digests the vendor engine, the training adapters and the controller sources; only exact, parity-verified implementation snapshots share a historical mechanics id (`simulator-compatibility.json`). `dataHash` digests the rankings, overrides and game master. `algorithmHash` digests the search code. A checkpoint resumes only under identical config, pool and algorithm; a warm start requires the same mechanics and meta and fresh evaluation seeds. Reports carry all three hashes, and the browser refuses to replay a report whose hashes do not match its adapter. A run with controller options is a different controller: its mechanics id is the v4 id plus a readable suffix (`+controller:replyModel=weighted,replyTemperature=0.25`), recorded with the options in `config.controller` and the report's `engineOptions`, so it never resumes, warm-starts, merges, exports or replays as the default controller, and the v4 adapter replays it only when the suffix matches those options.

**Mechanics: pinned, validated, unverified.** *Pinned:* `training/reference/mechanics-fixtures.json` holds 37 seeded battles from the pool (CMP ties at equal Attack, Shadow against regular, buff chances, Cramorant, Aegislash, Morpeko, Mimikyu, Darmanitan as a control, the switch clock, forced replacements, shields on both sides, identical teams) with every HP sample, timeline event, planner counter and observation record, and `mechanics-fixtures.test.cjs` requires exact replay in both traced and headless mode. `node training/mechanics-fixtures.cjs` reports any difference; `--write --reason "why"` accepts a reviewed change and records it in the file's changelog. `mechanics-mutation-check.cjs` applies eight deliberate edits to a copy of the engine, and each changes between 2 and 33 of the 37 battles. This is a regression net, not evidence that the game behaves the same. *Validated:* `tests/meta-benchmark/engine-vs-footage.cjs` compares the damage formula and move durations with the Meta Extractor's measurements from 21 videos. Charged hits agree with no bias (222 hits, measured / predicted median 0.98); fast hits agree within the bar-step noise (1,065 hits, 0.95); the extractor's formula and the engine's `DamageCalculator` give identical integer damage on every one of 2,502 sampled level/IV pairs. *Unverified:* equal-Attack CMP ties (a seeded coin per turn here), whether the game's CMP counts the Shadow bonus and stat stages (the emulator compares raw `CPM × (base + IV)`), the switch clock (45 s of emulator clock, running through cutscenes), catch and switch-animation timing, and buff application order; none can be read from other players' footage. Numbers, caveats and the recording and labelling plan are in [findings/mechanics-fidelity.md](findings/mechanics-fidelity.md).

## 6. The neural matchup model

![The pairwise matchup network: a shared encoder, a scalar strength and an antisymmetric interaction term](docs/teams-model.svg)

### Features

A team's input `x(T)` has 366 numbers: for each of the three members (lead first, bench in canonical order) the variant's features from scouting (typing, real Great League stats, CP and level, published score and default weight, fast-move type, damage per turn, energy per turn and duration, charged-move coverage, costs, power and proc chances, Shadow status, the scout profile against 24 opponents) plus 13 move-effect numbers (expected self and opponent attack/defence stage changes weighted by proc chance, and the Shadow flag), and then 36 team-level numbers: the best and worst scout coverage per defending type across the three members. Explicit coverage summaries help a small network learn synergy from few, expensive labels. Opponent fixtures are encoded the same way from their recommended movesets (with `--observed-moves`, from the observed movesets where recorded, partly approximated: section 4).

### Architecture

```
h(T) = tanh(W x(T) + b)            16 hidden units, shared encoder
s(T) = vᵀ h(T) + c                 scalar strength
P(A beats B) = σ( s(A) − s(B) + h(A)ᵀ K h(B) ),   K = −Kᵀ
```

The scalar term learns general strength; the antisymmetric interaction matrix learns counter relationships (A beats B, B beats C, C beats A), which a single strength number cannot represent. Swapping the teams gives exactly 1 − P, and identical inputs give 0.5. The model predicts expected matchup reward; a draw counts as half a win.

*Why pairwise.* The first model regressed a team's average score and did not beat a constant predictor. Learning individual matchup outcomes helped; adding the learned antisymmetric matrix improved held-out matchup error by a further 11–13% in the pilot. A cyclic-counter fixture confirms the model learns a relationship a scalar model cannot.

### Training

Binary cross-entropy on fractional targets (1, ½, 0) with Adam (rate 0.002, batches of 64, 6 epochs, tiny L2: 0.0001 on weights, 0.001 on K); gradients are checked numerically, including the constrained interaction parameters. Twelve independently initialised networks form the ensemble. Each fit samples at most 48,000 distinct labelled fixtures *without replacement* from the training set, then each member bootstraps that subset once. About 20% of candidate team identities (by hash) are excluded from every fit and serve as the held-out diagnostic set; screening and final fixtures never enter fitting.

Measured choices (three-seed comparisons on the pilot's 56,640 fixtures): six epochs instead of ten reduced held-out team-average error by 9.2% (per-fixture error slightly worse; team selection uses team averages, so six is the default); raising the fixture cap from 12,000 to 48,000 reduced it by 30.1%; sampling without replacement before bootstrapping reduced it by another 12.0% at a 24,000 cap; a whole-team bootstrap (resampling complete candidate blocks) worsened two of three folds by 12–14% and was not adopted; aligning buff features to charge-energy order made no difference; per-charged-move type indicators worsened error (0.0063 vs 0.0050) and were dropped.

### Inference and uncertainty

A team's estimated score is its mean P(A beats R) over 256 reference opponents R drawn once from the meta, averaged over the ensemble. Twelve workers score 12,000 candidates in about 1.5 s (8.4 s single-threaded), preserving every prediction exactly. The spread across the ensemble is used as a search heuristic for promising-but-uncertain candidates; it is **not** a calibrated confidence interval, because the members share training data and fixtures are correlated across candidates.

### Diagnostics

Every round records, on the held-out identities: per-fixture MSE of the neural prediction versus the constant mean, and team-mean MSE versus the constant mean. In the published v1 run the final values were 0.0041 versus 0.0173 (76.6% lower) for teams and 0.118 versus 0.163 for fixtures. Team RMSE of about 6.4 points is still larger than a small moveset difference, which is why the search retests contenders and the report refuses to call close finalists apart.

## 7. Search and acquisition

Each round (`search.cjs`, `propose.cjs`, `acquisition.cjs`):

1. **Generate 12,000 unseen candidates.** First every unseen single-member moveset substitution and both alternative leads around the eight highest-ranked incumbents (an exhaustive local neighbourhood of the retained variants, not of all moves); then, until the pool is full, 60% mutations of known leaders (35% swap a member's moveset, 20% change the lead, else replace a member) and 40% fresh random teams. Species Clause is enforced throughout and every team is canonicalised.
2. **Predict** all candidates with the ensemble.
3. **Select 24:** half the slots by highest predicted mean (`neural`), a quarter by highest mean + ensemble standard deviation (`uncertainty`, the "promising exploration" rule), a quarter uniform random teams independent of the proposal shortlist (`random`), and one retest: the incumbent every third round, otherwise one of the next seven contenders, or a moveset rival of a selected team on identical opponents.
4. **Simulate in two stages.** All 24 play the same 8 opponent fixtures; a deterministic optimistic score (mean shrunk toward 0.5 with 8 pseudo-fixtures, plus 1.28 posterior standard deviations, plus the ensemble spread) promotes the top half to the full 64 fixtures. That is 24×8 + 12×56 = 864 battles instead of 1,536, 43.75% fewer, so about 1.78× as many candidate starts per battle budget. The cost is that a strong team can be discarded after a noisy prefix (`--promotion-fraction 1` restores full allocation). Fixtures are dispatched common-prefix first, so comparisons keep matched observations if the process stops early; each result commits independently and the checkpoint is resumable.
5. **Refit** the ensemble, record the round's proposal-quality diagnostics (mean score of each source on the shared 8-fixture prefix) and held-out errors, checkpoint, and loop.

**Opt-in speed options** (off by default; the defaults, existing checkpoints and tests are unchanged).

- **Adaptive prefix length** (`--acquisition adaptive`, with `--acquisition-step 8`, `--promotion-fraction` and `--acquisition-margin 1.28`). Every candidate still plays the common first prefix, so the proposal-quality diagnostics stay comparable. After it, candidates grow `step` fixtures at a time. After each completed level a candidate gets an optimistic score (the promotion priority: shrunk mean plus 1.28 posterior standard deviations plus the ensemble spread) and a pessimistic score (shrunk mean minus 1.28 posterior standard deviations). It stops when its optimistic score is below the pessimistic score of the current k-th best candidate, k = ceil(fraction × candidates), or when it reaches the full panel. The margin replaces the 1.28 on both sides. Decisions use only completed levels, so they are a function of the recorded scores. The stage, level and active teams are saved in the pending round, so an interrupted round resumes to exactly the uninterrupted result, and fixtures are still dispatched common-prefix first. Each round's `history.jsonl` entry records the size of every level.
- **Shared retest panel** (`--retest-panel N`). A fixed panel of N opponent fixtures is drawn once from a guarded seed recorded in the checkpoint config, and is checked not to overlap the sealed screening and final fixtures. After each round's acquisition the retested team (source `retest`: the incumbent, a rotating contender or a moveset rival's parent) and the incumbent play the panel fixtures they have not played yet, so contenders accumulate paired evidence on common opponents. Panel scores are kept per team in the checkpoint; the observations are ordinary training labels tagged `retest-panel`. Each round's history entry, and `recommendations.json`, record the incumbent minus each contender on the shared panel (paired whole-fixture bootstrap; `recommendations.json` adds simultaneous bands).

**Training leaders** are ranked by a cautious selection score: shrink the fixture mean toward 0.5 with eight pseudo-fixtures, then subtract 1.28 posterior standard deviations. It reduces the chance that a lucky small sample leads, but it is a heuristic, not a confidence bound, and does not remove multiple-comparison optimism.

**Minimum evidence before leading** (`--min-lead-fixtures N`; default 0 = off, 64 recommended, the fully expanded panel). That ranking is the single ordering (`scoreRecords` in `search.cjs`) behind the training leader, the retest incumbent (`propose.cjs` `known[0]`, and with it the rotating contenders and the local neighbourhoods of the top eight) and the screening set. With N > 0 a team that has played fewer than N fixtures ranks after every team that has, whatever its score; inside each group the order is the cautious score as before. The screening set is then only teams at or above N, so it can be smaller than `--screen-teams` when few have reached N; if no team has reached N (a run too short to expand any team that far) the ranking is left as it was and the report says so. The threshold is recorded in the config, and the checkpoint (`selection`), `recommendations.json`, `report.html` and the portable report describe what it did. On the completed v4 run the rule at 64 would have changed nothing: although 50.8% of its 8,477 teams ended with only the 8-fixture screen, all 24 screened teams and the training leader in every one of 364 rounds already had at least 64 fixtures. At 128 it would have dropped 4 of the 24 (2 finalists). The screened teams' training scores exceeded their sealed screening scores by 7.9 points for teams with fewer than 128 fixtures (4 teams), 3.7 for 128 to 511 (11 teams) and 0.9 for 512 or more (9 teams): descriptive, small numbers, but the direction a lucky small sample would give ([findings/selection-protocol.md](findings/selection-protocol.md)).

**Budget.** Proposals stop at 80% of the wall-clock budget, or earlier once measured throughput shows that screening, final testing and baselines need more time (estimated after 512 battles with a 25% margin). The estimate includes two teams on the sealed confirmation panel (`--confirmation-opponents`, section 8). Duration-based runs exclude event-loop suspension gaps over 30 s (a sleeping laptop) and the battle watchdog allows 120 awake seconds.

**Evidence for the acquisition rule.** In the completed v3 run, pure-disagreement exploration proposals averaged 37.4% over 154 rounds, barely above random (34.0%), while neural exploitation proposals averaged 60.5%. A retrospective diagnostic on 757 held-out identities gave 32.9% for disagreement alone versus 60.0% for mean + disagreement. A fresh paired benchmark with the frozen model on 12,000 unseen candidates and a new 128-opponent panel (1,536 battles) gave 35.4% versus 54.2%: **+18.8 points, paired interval 13.6–24.1**. The local neighbourhoods and rotating retests passed regression tests but their independent contribution to final team quality has not been measured.

**Offline replay of the adaptive rule** (`audit-acquisition-offline.cjs`; no new simulation; method, imputation model and caveats in [findings/speed-acquisition.md](findings/speed-acquisition.md)). Both policies were replayed with the production decision functions on 122 published v1 batches (24 teams, all 64 fixtures recorded) and on 362 rounds of the completed v4 run, where the 12 teams the staged run did not promote have their unplayed fixtures imputed (44% of the cells). The staged rule's 864 battles per batch keep the true best three teams fully measured 93.4% (v1) and 88.9% (v4) of the time. The adaptive rule as specified (margin 1.28) keeps them about 100% of the time but spends 1,370 (v1) and 1,272 (v4) battles per batch, so it is a quality rule, not a saving: at equal spend it beats the staged curve by 0.0 to 0.4 points. With margin 0.5 and k = 6 it spends 856 (v1) and 811 (v4) battles and keeps the best three 97.5% and 94.8% of the time, which is +4.6 (interval 2.6 to 7.0) and +8.4 (7.0 to 9.9) points over the staged curve at the same spend. On a homogeneous batch (the run's 24 sealed-screening leaders) neither rule separates the teams and no difference from zero is distinguishable. These are batch-level recall figures on recorded outcomes; whether they give a better final team at equal wall-clock time is the open full-scale benchmark.

## 8. Evaluation protocol

1. **Screening.** The 24 best training teams by selection score each play the same 512 fresh opponent fixtures (a seed derived from the launcher's validation seed, checked against any absorbed evidence). Fixture-major scheduling keeps every contender on the same completed prefix if the deadline expires. The primary team and up to seven more finalists are chosen here.
2. **Final test.** The finalists and twelve fixed baseline teams (four uniform-random, four weight-sampled with each member's best scouted moveset, four greedy coverage teams built from scout ratings with different weak-link penalties and bounded coordinate swaps, all fixed before the test) play another common 512-fixture panel under a fresh seed. Each team's score is its mean over the panel.
3. **Comparisons.** The primary versus each baseline group uses a paired bootstrap over whole opponent fixtures (5,000 resamples, 95% interval), conditional on those baseline teams and the pinned simulator. The primary versus each other finalist uses a simultaneous paired bootstrap whose band accounts for comparing several finalists at once; a finalist is "unresolved" when its band crosses zero. The screening-selected primary stays the primary even if another finalist's final point estimate is higher.
4. **Reuse without contamination.** `absorb-evaluation.cjs` converts a finished run's screening, final and baseline battles into training evidence for a *new* run, marks their seeds as used, and the launcher then draws a fresh validation seed that must not collide. `merge.cjs` and `remap.cjs` combine or translate raw fixture observations only across identical pools and mechanics.
5. **Confirmation** (`--confirmation-opponents N`; default 256, 0 disables). If the final comparison finds a challenger clearly above the primary, choosing it would be choosing the best-looking of several teams on the very panel that revealed it. A challenger is a finalist whose simultaneous band against the primary lies entirely on its side (`challenger-better` in `finalistComparisons`) or a previous champion with the same verdict in `championComparisons` (a champion that is the primary again is a tie, not a challenger). The primary and every such challenger then play a new sealed panel of N fixtures: a seed derived from the validation seed by `confirmationSeed` (distinct from the screening, final, retest-panel and training seeds, which are guarded against it, and refused when absorbed evidence used it), fixtures checked against training evidence and every other panel, one common fixture list with the deadline-safe prefix rule, a paired bootstrap and one simultaneous band over every pair of the confirmation teams (`paired-stats.cjs`). The screening-selected primary stays the primary endpoint. The record, `confirmation`, says `challenger-confirmed` (a challenger's band is entirely on its side again), `not-confirmed` (the primary ahead or unresolved) or `incomplete` (no time), and shows each team's final-panel and confirmation scores. A confirmation-panel score was not used to choose its team, so it is an unbiased estimate; the same team's final-panel score is optimistic. Power is limited: at 256 fixtures and a paired sd of 0.45 one comparison resolves about 5.5 points, so "not confirmed" is not proof that there is no difference (use 512 or more to confirm smaller gaps). A challenger that only a longer final panel resolves (item 6) is added to the same sealed panel. The panel is evaluation evidence: absorbing the run records its seed in `evaluationEvidence`, and its battles never become training labels.
6. **Larger final panel** (`--validation-opponents-max M`; default = `--validation-opponents`, off). After screening, the final test and the confirmation, while wall time remains, the final common panel of finalists, baselines and previous champions grows in steps of 64 fixtures up to M (a share of the remaining time is kept back for a late confirmation). The fixture list is prefix-stable, so every earlier fixture keeps its score; each step plays only the new fixtures for every team, keeps only the common completed prefix if the deadline cuts it, recomputes the summaries and all comparisons and saves the checkpoint. `finalPanel` keeps the planned size in `requestedFixtures`, the completed total in `completedFixtures`, and records `extension` (planned maximum, each step, and why it stopped).
7. **Meta sensitivity (a read-out, after the final test).** The final-tested finalists are re-simulated under several alternative opponent metas on sealed panels of their own (section 4), and the report field `sensitivity` records each team's score per meta with its mean, min and spread (max − min). It does not change the primary or any comparison above: it says how much a finalist's score depends on the assumed opponents, against the spread that fixture noise alone would give. A simulator, controller or meta change is judged partly by it (below).
8. **Alternate-moveset stress panel (a read-out, after the confirmation and the policy panel, before the sensitivity panel).** `--alternate-panel N` (default 0 = off) replays the final-tested finalists on one sealed panel of N fixtures drawn from the run's own meta, three ways on the same opponent species, order and battle seeds: with the opponents as the run models them (recommended movesets, or the observed ones with `--observed-moves`), and with every opponent member on its best, then its second best, scouted alternate. A species' alternates are its retained pool variants ranked by 1-v-1 scout score, without the recommended moveset (a species with fewer alternates repeats its last). Per finalist the report records the three scores, each alternate minus the modelled score with a paired whole-fixture bootstrap band that is simultaneous over the three arms, the worst of the two changes, and how many members the swap changed (`alternatePanel`: checkpoint, `recommendations.json`, `report.html`, `findings.md` and the portable report, validated by `neural-report-core.js`, with a caption line on the finalists card). Its seed is derived from the run seed (offset 840001) and guarded against the screening, final, confirmation, policy-panel, retest-panel, reference, belief and baseline seeds; the sensitivity panel's seeds avoid it, absorbed evidence that used it is refused, and its fixtures are checked against the sealed panels and the training evidence. `budget.cjs` reserves its battles (finalists × N × 3) so it moves the end of training earlier and never shortens screening or the final panel, and a deadline keeps a common prefix of whole fixtures. `alternate-panel.cjs RUN_OR_REPORT` runs the same panel for any finished run's finalists, on a fresh seed that none of the sources used (section 13). It is a stress test and never affects selection: both alternates replace every opponent member at once, which a real opponent never does, so a drop is an upper bound on a moveset surprise, and the ranking is by 1-v-1 results, not by how often a moveset is played.

**Telling an improvement from noise.** Whether a change helped depends on its class, and the sealed final panel only answers part of the question. A pure speed or refactor change must reproduce byte-identical outcomes (same seeds, a fixed `--rounds`) and is then measured for throughput. A search, acquisition or model change under the same pool, meta, controller and seeds is judged by an equal-budget A/B: the two primaries are re-simulated together on one fresh panel with `rematch.cjs` (paired bootstraps, simultaneous bands over all pairs), alongside the `previous-champions` baseline group and matched-battle history curves from `compare-history.cjs`. The group is the earlier winners (the published v1 and v3 primaries and every completed run with the same pool hash, added automatically by `launch-next.cjs`; `--champions FILE[,FILE…]` elsewhere), re-simulated on the run's own sealed final panel, so each comparison is paired and unbiased. A rediscovered champion, or any difference inside the interval (about 4 points for one comparison and 5 for several at 512 fixtures), is a tie, not a failure. A simulator, controller or meta change relabels everything: scores are comparable only under the new assumptions, so the old champions are re-simulated there and the change itself is judged by realism metrics (mechanics-fidelity fixtures, and the report fields `policyPanel` and `sensitivity` that are being added; they are not in reports yet), never by a higher score. Including the previous victors is enough only when simulator, meta and pool are unchanged and they are compared with the new primary on the same sealed panel. See [EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md](EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md) for the decision procedure and the statistical fine print, and [findings/selection-protocol.md](findings/selection-protocol.md) for the confirmation test, the longer panel and the retrospective on the v4 run.

**Where the time goes.** A profile of the simulator attributed about 15% to type-effectiveness tables, 6% each to move creation, Pokémon construction, garbage collection and move resetting, 4% to move initialisation and 5% to damage; the dominant cost in v4 is repeated forecast preparation, since every switching decision creates duels and prepares six Pokémon per imagined lineup. The implemented fixes share immutable ordered type tables, cache normalised move metadata and public stat templates, prepare only selected attacks for forecast Pokémon, reuse damage-only opponent guesses within a turn and identical branches across both controllers (branch cache), and run forecasts headlessly. A 2026-10-13 profile of 48 search-shaped battles still put 62% of the time in forecast branches and 29% in building forecast Pokémon (six per branch, plus one per guessed-opponent check). Global name lookups in the contextified VM cost about 350 ns each. Forecast reuse cut the forecast Pokémon built by 73% and duel simulations by 33%. The largest remaining costs are PvPoke's own AI and state machine inside forecast duels (about 13%), building the two fresh duel members (11%), garbage collection (12%), belief-world construction (6%) and per-encounter VM setup (6–9%).

**Measured and rejected:** longer worker lifetimes (8 vs 24 or 32 battles: no gain, more memory); caching compiled simulator scripts across contexts (no gain); larger heaps; transporting the public prior once per worker; flattened inference reference data (slower than reusable scratch buffers); fewer forecast worlds or shorter horizons (they change decisions, so they are not transparent optimisations); exact recycling of forecast Pokémon from a learned layout (exact, but 0.92× because a restore copies about as much state as a build); skipping repeated resets (PvPoke's move-preference reset is not idempotent). The GPU and Neural Engine are not used: the branching simulator is not a tensor workload and the network is tiny.

## 11. The viewer

The Team Rankings tab loads the published v1 archive by default and can import any `report.pvpteams.json.gz` (data only; the report never carries executable code). It shows:

- **Teams:** every evaluated team with training score, cautious selection score, screening score and fresh-test score; 500 per page; search with the rankings syntax where each AND term may match a different member (`melmetal&cramorant&@counter`), `lead:` scopes a predicate to the lead, negation excludes teams; Lead and alphabetical Backline columns; Min, Max and Average published rank; an Alternate Moveset badge against the published recommendation.
- **Battles:** each team's paired battle list by stage; clicking a fixture reruns both orientations in a Web Worker with the report's engine version, seed, controller and belief priors, verifies the replay against the stored score, and graphs all six Pokémon's HP with attack, shield, switch and faint markers. Replay is allowed only when the report's mechanics and data hashes match the installed adapter.
- **Team Comparison:** two teams on their freshest common stage: paired score deltas, an approximate 95% interval, changed outcomes, conditional opponent associations, side-by-side replays, and an optional matched exploratory panel of up to 512 fixtures. Comparison replays list the charged attacks actually used and flag changed moves that never fired.
- **Search Performance:** proposal quality (trailing means of neural, random and uncertainty picks), the training leader's raw score, held-out MSE against the constant baseline, simulation progress, the final baseline comparison (with the previous-champions group and a champion-by-champion table when the run had one), finalist cards (each says whether the sealed confirmation test confirmed it, did not, or was not needed), a Sealed confirmation test table (final-panel and confirmation scores side by side, simultaneous intervals, verdicts), a fixture counter that names the planned larger final panel, and the opponent-meta line ("Opponent meta: 75% observed teams, 25% PvPoke prior · 190 teams (181 distinct) from 18 creators …"). When the report has a `sensitivity` block (runs from 2026-10-13 on) it adds a meta-sensitivity table (teams across the alternative metas, with the final-test score for reference, mean, min, spread and the spread fixture noise alone would give) and one line under each finalist's bar: "across 6 metas: min 62.5%, spread 14.1 points". A report with an `alternatePanel` block adds a caption under the finalists chart with the primary's score against the modelled and the alternate opponent movesets; opponents with observed movesets replay with those moves.

The report contract (`format: "pvp-team-report"`, schema 1, engine versions v1–v4) is documented in [NEURAL_REPORTS.md](../../includes/pro/NEURAL_REPORTS.md).

## 12. Gaps and improvement areas

### Accuracy (how much the result can be trusted)

| Gap | Evidence | What would close it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The target distribution** | the optimiser finds a best response to its opponents; the synthetic prior's weight-1 tail and hand-designed cores are assumptions; the observed export has grown from 190 teams by 18 creators to 540 teams by 35, but the first export had 43% of members with unknown Shadow status. **Since 2026-10-13:** every run re-simulates its finalists under 4 to 6 alternative metas (`sensitivity`, section 4), `sensitivity.cjs` does the same for any finished run, and the observed export can be cut by date (`--since`, `--until`). First measurement, exploratory (the 8 finalists of the v4 observed run, 3 metas, 64 fixtures each, 4 workers on a shared machine): scores of 75 to 92% on every meta; mean spread 7.3 points against a fixture-noise floor of 7.4, so no finalist's spread is clearly above noise; no pair of finalists resolved on any meta (simultaneous band ±16 to 19 points); the primary ranked 5th, 1st and 7th of 8 on the three metas. Rating strata are impossible: no battle or video record in the catalog holds an opponent rating or bracket | **Done:** the panel, the standalone tool, date strata. **Remaining:** the full-scale panel on the completed run (512 fixtures per meta; commands in [findings/meta-sensitivity.md](findings/meta-sensitivity.md)); more harvested battles and answered Shadow questions; a source of opponent skill (new extraction work, such as reading the rank or rating shown on the result screen); a date-stratified comparison (early against late window) once the archive spans more than October 2026 (533 of 538 rows are from that month); a test of whether a robust objective (mean minus spread) selects better teams, which needs replicated panels; metas that vary the opponents' movesets |
| **The controller** | bounded minimax switching with a heuristic terminal value, simple attack and shield rules, perfect counting; a 128-fixture check shows parity with the old controller, not strength. Since 2026-10-13 (opt-in, defaults byte-identical on 216 before/after fixtures): softmax-weighted replies (`--reply-model weighted`) scored 50.0% head to head against the default (64 fixtures, 46.9–53.1%) and changed 15 of 128 battles at T = 0.25; rollout continuation (`--rollout-continuation 8`) scored 53.5% (46.5–60.6%), changed 94 of 128 battles and costs about 1.16× CPU per battle. A policy-diversity panel (`--policy-panel N`, `policyPanel` in reports) replays finalists against six opponent styles: on the 2026-10-12 finalists (32 fixtures per style, exploratory) the default v4 controller was the hardest style for five of eight, and no style was resolved below it. All of this is smoke-scale on a shared machine ([findings](findings/controller.md)) | the 512-fixture head-to-heads and a 256-fixture panel (commands in the findings); a reply temperature fitted to human choices; continuation beyond one replacement, delayed catches, a learned terminal value; stronger and human-derived opponent styles for the panel; model counting errors and lag |
| **Opponent movesets** | by default opponents run recommended movesets, so counters that rely on an alternate move and bait patterns around it are absent. **Since 2026-10-13:** the observed export records the moves the harvest saw (2026-10-13 export, 2,349 opponent members: a fast move for 75.5%, at least one charged move for 45.5%; the observed fast move differs from the recommended one for 3.0% of those, a charged move outside the recommended pair for 10.4%, and 6.9% of members differ in some way), `--observed-moves` plays them in the observed component (19.3% of observed team draws contain a differing member, 70 distinct observed movesets, 37 of them kept by the pool), and `--alternate-panel N` replays the finalists with every opponent member on its best and second best scouted alternate. First measurement, exploratory (the 8 finalists of the completed 2026-10-12 run re-simulated with `alternate-panel.cjs` on 128 common fixtures of the hybrid meta from the 2026-10-13 export, 4 workers, shared machine): with the observed movesets as the opponents' moves, 1 to 3 of the 128 outcomes differ per finalist (mean change −0.5 points, none beyond ±2.3); with every opponent member on its best or second best alternate the changes run from −7.0 to +5.1 points and none is resolved (simultaneous bands of about ±10 points). The primary lost 2.3 and 0.8 points against the two alternates with the recommended opponents, unresolved. One panel, fewer than 256 fixtures, no accuracy claim; the smoke search path also runs end to end, and 60 of 60 saved matches replay from the report alone | **Done:** the export, the validated loader, the identity and model handling, the stress panel. **Remaining:** the full-scale runs (commands in [findings/opponent-movesets.md](findings/opponent-movesets.md)) and a comparison of the primary chosen with and without `--observed-moves`; frequencies of each moveset per species rather than the sets of single battles (the file holds one set per battle, so a species seen once fixes its set for that fixture); measuring how much the extractor's fast-move inference leans on the recommendation (the 3.0% may be too low) and answering the 24% of fast moves under .90; a belief prior that learns moveset frequencies without leaking the fixture's set; observed movesets in the sensitivity metas and the policy panel (`rematch.cjs` and `alternate-panel.cjs` take `--observed-moves`); a stress panel that changes one member at a time instead of all three |
| **Retained movesets and IVs** | five 1-v-1-scouted movesets per entry; default IV builds; a team-specific moveset can be excluded | targeted moveset mutation outside the five with 3-v-3 retention; alternate IV builds for the finalists |
| **Surrogate error and calibration** | team RMSE about 6 points; ensemble spread is not an interval; held-out identities come from the same search and meta | candidate, opponent, policy and meta holdouts; proper uncertainty needs both grouping dimensions and independent simulation |
| **Selection and multiple comparisons** | eight finalists with overlapping bands; the primary is pre-selected by screening, but a challenger that tops the final panel is the best of several noisy looks, and a lucky small sample can reach screening. On the completed v4 run no challenger was clearly above the primary (all seven bands unresolved), so no confirmation would have run (one finalist taken as the primary in turn would have triggered it); the minimum-evidence rule at 64 would have changed nothing (all 24 screened teams and every round's leader had at least 64 fixtures; at 128 it would drop 4 of 24); screened teams with fewer than 128 fixtures scored 7.9 points lower in sealed screening than in training, those with 512 or more 0.9 | **Built 2026-10-13, smoke-tested only:** `--min-lead-fixtures` (thin teams cannot lead, be the incumbent or be screened), a sealed 256-fixture confirmation test for any resolved challenger or previous champion (the primary stays the endpoint; the report says confirmed or not), and `--validation-opponents-max` (final panel grown in steps of 64 while time remains). Still open: a full-scale run with them (commands in [findings/selection-protocol.md](findings/selection-protocol.md)); whether 128 or a 1,024-fixture panel changes the winner (needs replicated runs); 256 fixtures cannot confirm gaps under about 5 points; screening itself is not repeated |
| **Telling a real improvement from noise** | a run's own final score is conditional on its panel and assumptions (the v1 primary's 68.3% became 42.2% on the observed meta under the v4 controller in an exploratory 64-fixture rematch); one run per arm gives no estimate of search-to-search variance; at 512 fixtures differences under about 4 to 5 points are unresolved | the tools now exist: `previous-champions`, `rematch.cjs`, `compare-history.cjs` and [EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md](EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md). Still open: replicated seeds per arm, time-to-champion (history records scores, not team identity), 1,024 or more fixtures for sub-3-point claims, and the realism metrics (`policyPanel`, `sensitivity`) for assumption changes |
| **Mechanics fidelity** | PvPoke's emulator approximations are inherited; canonical routing removes caller-order artefacts, not game differences. **Pinned:** 37 seeded battles replay exactly (CMP ties, buff chances, forms, switch clock, forced replacements, Shadows), and 8 of 8 deliberate mechanics edits change them. **Validated against footage:** the damage formula fits 222 measured charged hits (measured / predicted median 0.98, IQR 0.92-1.05) and 1,065 fast hits (0.95, 0.76-1.18, limited by bar-step noise), and the extractor's copy of it is identical to the engine's on 2,502 level/IV pairs; hit spacing favours the labelled move duration (37% against 8% for the other durations) but is noisy. **Unverified:** how the game breaks CMP ties, whether CMP counts the Shadow bonus and stat stages, the switch clock (45 s of emulator clock, 60 s on screen), catch and switch-animation timing, buff application order | recordings and labels as planned in [findings/mechanics-fidelity.md](findings/mechanics-fidelity.md): equal-IV friendly mirror matches, 100 labelled voluntary-switch pairs, same-turn exchange labels; review every mechanics change with `mechanics-fixtures.cjs --write --reason` and keep the mechanics hash honest |
| **Coverage of the space** | 3,215 canonical identities seen in 3,453 records, 0.00019% of the pool | this is inherent; the honest claim is "a strong team under these assumptions", never "the optimal team" |

### Speed (how much search fits in a budget)

| Gap | Evidence | What would close it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forecast preparation dominates** | partly addressed: branch caching and headless mode recovered 1.33×. Forecast reuse (2026-10-13) shares duel forecasts across belief worlds and reuses bench and guessed-opponent clones (73% fewer forecast Pokémon built), with an ordinary VM global, for 1.9–2.0× more battles per second at smoke scale and identical outcomes. Forecasts still spend most of their time in PvPoke's AI and in building fresh duel members | the 12-worker benchmark ([findings](findings/speed-forecast.md)); an array-based combat state instead of Pokémon objects (a larger rewrite needing full timing and form parity); exact pooling of forecast state did not pay (a restore copies as much as a build) |

Offline replay (section 7): the adaptive rule at its specified margin costs 47 to 59% more battles per batch than staged for about 100% best-three recall; with margin 0.5 and k = 6 it matches staged spend (811 to 856) at +4.6 to +8.4 points of best-three recall. | **Fitting and inference are already small** | 20–25 s per fit, 1.5 s per inference round against hours of simulation | no action needed until the simulator is several times faster |
| **Pool preparation** | 18.4 M simulations, cached | unchanged data snapshot reuses the pool; a new snapshot pays once |

## 13. Reproducibility and files

Within a run directory: `pool.json` (population, variants, stats, scouting settings, input and pool hashes), `checkpoint.json` (raw fixture labels, models, pending round, diagnostics, screening, final and baseline results, telemetry; atomic after every batch), `history.jsonl` (round summaries), `models.json`, `recommendations.json`, `opponent-analysis.json` (every primary final fixture with opponent movesets and seeds), `report.html` / `findings.md`, `report.pvpteams.json.gz`, `training.log`, `launch.json` and `process.json` (verified process identity, start and end, deadline).

Compatibility rules: resume requires the original algorithm code, settings and pool (worker count and hours may change; ensemble size and seeds are fixed); warm start requires the same mechanics and meta and fresh evaluation seeds; v1–v3 labels never warm-start v4 (`--seed-teams OLD_CHECKPOINT` passes compositions as initial hints only, re-simulated); historical reports keep their original mechanics ids and replay with their own adapters.

With the selection protocol the checkpoint, `recommendations.json` (`confirmation`, `selection`, `finalPanel.extension`), `report.html`, `findings.md` and the portable report (`confirmation`, `search.minLeadFixtures`, `finalPanel.extension`; validated by `neural-report-core.js`) record what it did; the checkpoint also keeps the confirmation panel's per-fixture scores and fixtures (`fixturePanels[confirmationSeed]`). A run interrupted during the confirmation panel resumes by replaying the final stage from the saved training state (seeded battles, identical results) and leaves no half-written record.

`sensitivity.cjs` takes a run directory, `recommendations.json` or portable report, maps the finalists into the pool (as `rematch.cjs` does), builds the alternative metas from the run's own usage settings (`launch.json`, `meta` in the checkpoint; flags override) and its belief prior (the checkpoint or the report's `engineOptions`), draws fresh sealed seeds that no source used, and prints the table with a simultaneous band per meta (`paired-stats.cjs`); `--print` re-reads a saved result. `rematch.cjs` takes run directories, `recommendations.json`, portable reports or the v1 archive, maps each composition into the pool, draws a fresh panel (refusing a seed a source used), and prints every pair under one simultaneous band; `--finalists` adds all final-tested teams. `compare-history.cjs` reads two `history.jsonl` files at equal cumulative battles and reports neural-proposal quality, held-out team error against the constant predictor, best training score, teams labelled and battles per labelled team, each as improvement, no difference or regression.
