How the team search works

This model searches for three-Pokémon teams and their movesets. Each label comes from a complete three-on-three battle using PvPoke’s existing Champion strategy on both sides. It learns team strength and counter relationships; it does not learn a new switching or catching strategy.

1. Eligible Pokémon and movesets

The pool contains the top 300 Great League entries by positive Default PvPoke weight (rank breaks ties). Forms are separate entries; a team cannot contain two entries with the same Pokédex number. Up to five movesets per entry are retained from exhaustive one-on-one scouting of all legal combinations across five shield/energy scenarios against the 300 recommended opponents. Those simulations use weighted Battle Rating.

2. Full-team training data

Opposing teams are sampled without replacement by Pokédex number, proportional to PvPoke Default weights, using recommended movesets. Each fixture is played with the two teams swapping sides. A win earns 1, a draw ½, and a loss 0. Team order is retained, so the lead matters. Opponents are independently sampled teams, rather than observed human lineups.

3. Neural matchup model

Twelve bootstrapped neural networks each use 366 team features and a 16-unit tanh hidden layer. Features describe typing, bulk, published scores and weights, move damage, energy, duration, coverage, effects, and team order. Adam fits binary cross-entropy for six epochs on up to 48,000 distinct fixtures per network.

h(T) = tanh(Wx(T) + b)
s(T) = vᵀh(T) + c
P(A,B) = sigmoid(s(A) − s(B) + h(A)ᵀKh(B))
K = −Kᵀ

The antisymmetric interaction term can represent cyclic counters. Swapping teams gives complementary predictions. Roughly 20% of candidate team identities are held out from fitting for diagnostics. Ensemble disagreement guides exploration; it is not a calibrated confidence interval.

4. Search and selection

Each round scores 12,000 proposed teams: 60% mutations of leaders and 40% fresh random teams. It simulates 24: twelve neural picks, six uncertainty picks, five random picks, and one leader retest. Each plays 64 fresh opponent fixtures on both sides. Neural predictions are averaged over 256 fixed reference opponents.

A cautious training selection score favors consistent evidence:

n = games / 2
m = (reward / 2 + 4) / (n + 8)
selection = m − 1.28 × √(m × (1 − m) / (n + 9))

This is a selection heuristic, not a formal confidence bound. The graph shows the raw training score of the leader selected by this heuristic, a separate diagnostic that can be optimistic.

5. Independent evaluation

Twenty-four leaders enter screening; the top eight then play 512 fresh opponent fixtures on both sides (1,024 battles each). The primary team is chosen by screening before final testing. Four teams per baseline method play the same final opponents. Comparison intervals use a paired opponent-fixture bootstrap and are conditional on these baseline teams. Pilot evaluation evidence was absorbed into training; current screening and test seeds are fresh.

Comparing teams

Select two report checkboxes and click Compare Teams, or compare the selected team with its opponent below a simulated battle. Shared results match the exact opponent order and random seed; the freshest common stage is selected. The report shows paired score differences, an approximate 95% interval, changed outcomes, opponent associations and side-by-side replays. Different training panels are not compared as if they were identical. If shared fixtures are unavailable, run a matched exploratory panel of up to 512 fixtures. Identical alphabetical bench order can isolate moveset changes when the lead and species are the same. Original saved simulator order remains unchanged.

Lead is a separate column. Bench Pokémon display alphabetically; Min, Max and Average Ranking summarize published Great League species ranks (lower is better). Search lead:melmetal, lead:@counter, or lead:melmetal/@thunder shock to restrict a query to the lead. Comparison replays list the charged attacks actually used and flag changed moves that were never fired. Conditional opponent trends are associations, not causal 1-v-1 conclusions.

Reading the graphs

Time starts at the unattended run, which resumed pilot evidence. Proposal quality uses a five-round trailing average of actual simulated scores. Held-out MSE measures team-mean prediction error; lower is better, and the comparison predicts one constant mean. Training and held-out diagnostics are not final validation scores. Finalist cards show fresh test outcomes; test scores were measured once at the end.

What the result means

The leader was already found in the pilot, and this run confirmed it. These are simulation scores under fixed, full-information AI and a weighted opponent distribution, not human win rates or proof of an optimal team. Restricting each Pokémon to five individually scouted movesets can miss useful team-specific combinations. CPU workers run the battle engine; this run did not use the GPU or Neural Engine.

How moveset selection works

Recommended ranks the published moveset for every accepted Pokémon.

Multiple first shortlists combinations by type-aware damage and energy cycles, then scouts five battle scenarios against up to eight opponents chosen for weight and type coverage. The recommended moveset reserves one of the selected slots. If fewer combinations exist than your maximum, all are retained.

Scouting models energy, damage, shields and timing, but cannot guarantee the full-roster best moveset. Move filters test what a Pokémon can learn; they do not restrict ranked movesets.

How calculated weights work

This is an estimated strength-based meta, not measured usage. PvPoke guidance preserves its curated priorities while simulations adapt to your selected roster.

Weights of 0 or 1 get a steep performance taper, so hundreds of fringe Pokémon do not each contribute a full 1×. Every Pokémon remains eligible with a tiny positive floor.

Recommended movesets battle in all five scenarios. We repeatedly measure performance against the current weighted opponents, blend that with the PvPoke prior, and damp updates until stable or the iteration limit is reached. More variants do not create extra opponents.

Calculated weights replace the existing iterative meta multiplier in category scoring, preventing the estimate from being applied twice. Other score rules remain in place.
